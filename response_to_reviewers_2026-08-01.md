# Response to Reviewer

Manuscript: *Coalition Design under Heterogeneous AV Objectives: Regimes and Low-Regret Selection in Mixed-Autonomy Networks*

We thank the reviewer for the detailed and constructive report. The revision
now has a coalition-first hierarchy. The paper asks how heterogeneous
management objectives across AV companies, and the coalition structure that
aggregates those objectives, change equilibrium congestion when aggregate
demand and physical road costs are fixed. The OD-restricted response and HDV
screening operators are retained as the transmission mechanism. HHI is a
descriptive mass statistic and is no longer presented as the principal object.

## Major comment 1: coalition semantics and objective heterogeneity

**Response.** The revised model treats a coalition as a joint routing
decision-maker whose members retain their accepted OD obligations. Company
heterogeneity enters through the convex management objective for each company's
AVs, not through company-specific road physics. The coalition governance matrix
has company-specific diagonal curvature and cross-member terms controlled by
$\gamma$. We distinguish identity-preserving coordination from operational
pooling because pooling enlarges the feasible set and would otherwise confound
objective aggregation with demand reassignment.

## Major comment 2: relation to restricted sensitivity analysis

**Response.** The bordered inverse and OD-restricted sensitivity tools are
positioned as classical foundations (Tobin and Friesz, 1988; Qiu and Magnanti,
1989; Patriksson, 2004; Josefsson and Patriksson, 2006). The paper's added
composition is coalition-specific OD projection, edge-space aggregation, and
the quotient by the endogenous HDV Wardrop adjustment space. Under affine
costs, fixed supports, and positive curvature on feasible tangents, the
activated signature $(\mathcal A_HR_\Pi,\mathcal A_Hs_\Pi)$ is sufficient for
aggregate equilibrium. Equality of signatures is necessary for equality of the
full conditional response map on an open perturbation set; equality at one
right-hand side is not claimed to be necessary.

## Major comment 3: HHI and ownership coarsening

**Response.** HHI is now explicitly auxiliary. The central comparisons vary
coalition composition and coalition degree, not HHI alone. The paper retains
fixed-HHI spatial portfolios as a diagnostic showing that equal masses and equal
HHI can still yield different congestion when OD exposure changes. We do not
claim that HHI is useless; we claim that it is not a sufficient statistic for
the equilibrium state generated by heterogeneous objectives and governance.

## Major comment 4: objective and governance sensitivity

**Response.** We add a certified sensitivity package varying low/high objective
curvature designs, coordination strength $\gamma\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}$, and
the absolute reference management slope in
$\{0.5\bar b,\bar b,2\bar b\}$. The primary estimand is the
assortative-minus-mixed recovery contrast, accompanied by $\Delta J/J^{UE}$,
its sign over $\gamma$, and the relation to the low/high objective gap. These
experiments test whether the composition result is tied to one numerical
calibration or persists over the declared mechanism family.

## Major comment 5: systematic scan, regime theory, and policy value

**Response.** We add all 15 set partitions of four companies at two AV
penetrations and two positive coordination levels, for 60 certified Sioux
Falls profiles. The scan reports partition-level recovery and TSTT, coalition
count and HHI, Spearman and Kendall association with coalition HHI, pairwise
inversion rates, recovery dispersion, and objective-composition class means.
We now use this enumeration only as an audit baseline, rather than treating the
ranked list as the research conclusion. Near ties are handled with a set-valued
optimum; at $\alpha=0.9,\gamma=0.5$, two symmetry-equivalent three--one
partitions differ by only $5.58\times10^{-7}$ TSTT units.

The substantive upgrade has three parts. First, a general-network merge score
equals the directional derivative of TSTT along a fixed-support governance
path. Second, a canonical bottleneck theorem identifies HDV-screened,
boundary-response, and interior-response regimes, including a threshold at
$\Sigma^*=1-2H/x^{UE}$ under which a partial coalition can dominate both
endpoints. Third, we evaluate local merge policies by policy regret. One-step
merging is exact in all four Sioux Falls states. Across 32 states on independent
Braess and directed-grid networks, it is exact in 29 states with mean regret
$0.332$ recovery points; two-step lookahead is exact in all 32. We report the
three one-step failures and state explicitly that two-step exactness is a
benchmark result, not a universal theorem.

The cross-network design contains 480 certified profiles. Both networks prefer
the grand coalition at all tested states with $\alpha\leq0.7$, then enter a
partial-coordination regime at $\alpha=0.9$ under sufficiently strong
governance. The transition boundary differs by topology. These are systematic
within-benchmark regime and magnitude statements, not market-population
estimates.

We also add an explicit governance burden. Pairwise loss thresholds give the
value of coordination at which the selected organization switches, and the
lower envelope can contain grand, partial, and fragmented intervals. This
separates congestion benefits from managerial, informational, or contracting
resources needed to implement cross-company governance.

## Major comment 6: identity-preserving coordination versus operational pooling

**Response.** Proposition~\ref{prop:pooling} proves
$\mathcal Y_C^{\mathrm{id}}\subseteq\mathcal Y_C^{\mathrm{pool}}$. We add a small
two-penetration experiment that uses the same coalition objective, company
masses, aggregate OD demand, and coalition partition under both feasible sets.
When member OD portfolios and route access are proportional, the two induced
aggregate equilibria coincide up to numerical tolerance, as the proposition
predicts. This is reported as a mechanism decomposition, not as evidence that
pooling is always neutral. A nonproportional spatial-portfolio extension is
reserved for the next scan if pooling is to be evaluated as a separate policy
intervention.

## Major comment 7: Nash interpretation and equilibrium well-posedness

**Response.** AV coalitions are atomic splittable players minimizing their
perceived convex management objective conditional on other flows. HDVs are a
nonatomic Wardrop population. The revised existence and monotonicity results
apply to continuous nondecreasing physical costs and PSD quadratic management
terms. Aggregate flow is unique on edges whose physical cost is strictly
increasing between equilibria. The affine activated closed form remains
support-conditional; it is not asserted as a global BPR formula.

## Major comment 8: BPR and numerical certification

**Response.** BPR results are separated from the exact affine theorem. The
coalition quadratic term is PSD by construction, so the general VI operator is
monotone under nondecreasing BPR costs. The earlier perceived-slope Jacobian
condition is retained only as a pointwise sufficient-region diagnostic. Every
reported Sioux Falls profile is independently checked by a normalized
all-player VI gap and a full-network player-specific omitted-route oracle.
The composition, degree, sensitivity, partition, spatial, penetration, and
pooling packages report their raw records, summaries, scripts, and validators.

## Major comment 9: support conditioning and computational scope

**Response.** The activated signature is not presented as an ex ante scalar
index. It compresses repeated organizational comparisons within a verified
support. The computational workflow solves the reduced system, checks active
flow positivity and omitted-route inequalities, and updates the route support
when necessary. This complements, rather than replaces, support enumeration
and parametric equilibrium computation.

## Scope and contribution statement

The revised paper has three contributions: a coalition-structured mixed
Nash--Wardrop model with heterogeneous AV management objectives; a response
regime theory combining an activated equilibrium representation, a
general-network merge score, and a canonical analytic threshold; and a
regret-based local selection framework validated on Sioux Falls, Braess, and a
directed grid. The full partition scans audit the policy and diagnose why HHI
cannot replace it. The paper studies the congestion consequences of specified
coalition structures. It does not claim endogenous coalition stability,
universal optimality of two-step search, a global nonlinear BPR regime theorem,
external calibration of the controlled objective types or governance burden,
pricing or induced demand, or population prevalence beyond the declared
benchmark designs.
